## Summary
- The book is split into 12 chapters each arguing a different point a government should make. In classic Milton Friedman fashion the writing is very dense and you have to read every page several times to be able to digest it. Hell, I'll have to read the book another time.

- The arguments Friedman makes are very unconventional like he believes that we should abolish the standard for doctors to have medical degrees as it prohibits people who couldn't get a medical degree but are otherwise good doctors to practice. And doing that would also lower cost, he argues that people don't check if a doctor has a medical degree anyways and go with word of mouth or reviews.

- He also argues that education should be privatized in areas where there isn't a technical monopoly as private companies will give a much better education than the state for a lower cost.

- He mentions a funny point that after the great depression the Federal reserve was established to prevent further depressions but since then more depressions have occured so he argues for less government control over the economy.


## Notes

- I really liked this book to be honest though Friedman's style will take some time to get used to, and this is my first economics book to read that isn't a textbook so I have a lot to learn still.

- I'll definitely re-read this book again.
