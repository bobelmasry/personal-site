## Summary
- The book starts off with Winston, an unhealthy 50 ish year old man working for the Ministry of Truth in Oceania in London who has varicose veins.

- Winston is repulsed by everyone around him and doesn't really have any friends but lives in the Victory Apartments with his only source of joy the Victory Cigarettes and the Victory Chocolate. His job is to modify the past so that it aligns with what the government is currently saying as they use the quote "who controls the past controls the future and he who controls the present controls the past".

- The political parties around the world are all Totalitarion which prohibit oppositional parties or any form of internal conflict and try to control their inhabitants' lives as much as they can through devices like the "telescreen", a sort of tv which records all the time and notifies the government if you're doing anything foul. 

- Winston eventually becomes more oppositional and critical of the party and starts getting involved with a younger woman called Julia, who even though she's a member of the anti-sex league boasts that she slept with many men including some from the inner party and that was her form of rebellion. Winston and Julia start meeting more often which is dangerous as their relationship isn't sanctioned by the party so they have to meet secretely and in remote places to have sex. And over time they rent a room from Mr. Charrington near a prole neighbourhood to start spending more time together.

- Winston then starts to realise that the only truly free people in Oceania are the proles who account for around 80% of the population (uneducated people who are allowed to drink beer and get married without the party's permission) and eventually gets the book by Emmanual Goldstein (the supposed leader of the so-called oppositional party) from O'brien a collegue of Winston's whom they thought were part of the oppositional party. 

- A short while later both Julia and Winston get caught in the room they rented by Mr. Charrington who turned out to be an undercover agent and get sent to the ministry of love where Winston gets brainwashed into believing all the lies the party tells its people by O'brien through electrocution. Winston finally leaves prison and becomes a completely different person who only believes what the party tells him is true and works now at a much higher paying job though without any responsibilites.


## Notes

- I read this book a couple of years ago and even had a physical copy but I didn't remember much of it so I reread it. I found the depiction of this dystopian world fascinating and how vivid Orwell describes it and how lifelike it could've been in an alternate universe.

- Though I found the chapter near the end where Winston's reading the book by Goldstein incredibly dry where it was just Orwell describes his political ideology and I felt that it wasn't really relevant to the book.
